I want to start by narrowing the mission before we jump into features.

The brief says to build a trustworthy finance-operations assistant for a bounded spend or expense workflow. My current hypothesis is that the biggest opportunity is **not to build a general “AI finance assistant,” but to pick one high-friction decision in the spend lifecycle where policy interpretation, approvals, and auditability are currently slow or inconsistent.**

A possible starting problem could be:

> **Employees and approvers spend too much time figuring out whether an expense is compliant, who needs to approve it, and why it was approved or rejected.**

The product hypothesis would then be:

> If we can interpret company policy, evaluate an expense against it, identify the right approval path, and produce a clear evidence-backed explanation and audit trail, we can reduce manual review effort without asking finance teams to give up control.

Before we commit to this, I think we should challenge a few assumptions together:

* **Where is the real pain?** Is it policy checking, approval routing, exception handling, reimbursement review, or something else?
* **Who feels the pain most strongly?** Employees submitting expenses, managers approving them, or finance/operations teams auditing them?
* **What should AI actually decide versus only recommend?** For a trustworthy first version, I am leaning toward AI recommending and explaining, with deterministic rules and human approval for consequential actions.
* **What evidence would make us change direction?** For example, if interviews show that policy interpretation is already easy but approval follow-up is the real bottleneck, we should change the workflow rather than force the original idea.
* **What is the smallest workflow we can validate with 3–5 real users within three weeks?**

I also want us to avoid assuming that because Ramp has AI features, our product needs to resemble Ramp. The goal should be to find a narrower problem where we can show a measurable improvement and a credible reason for a customer to care.

@dmondal @pchouksey @nigupta — Adding the engineering evidence behind this product decision.
ClearSpend now has a reproducible containerized architecture with separate Next.js, FastAPI, Procrastinate worker, PostgreSQL, encrypted MinIO storage and ClamAV services. LangGraph remains a bounded library inside the worker rather than an independent autonomous service.
Hypothesis → evidence → decision: We hypothesized that deterministic finance controls must remain authoritative while AI handles only ambiguity. During the architecture audit, we found that receipt checks and receipt-security flags were being aggregated after the optional AI step. We changed the flow so receipt evidence enters the graph first, suspicious receipt content skips the model, deterministic failures retain rejection precedence, and unknown results require human review.
Verification evidence: Ruff, mypy, frontend lint/type checks, Compose validation and frontend tests passed; 38 backend tests passed, with two PostgreSQL-specific tests configured for CI. Decision: retain the modular monolith, PostgreSQL-backed durable worker and bounded human-in-the-loop assessment rather than introducing autonomous agents or additional infrastructure without evidence.
