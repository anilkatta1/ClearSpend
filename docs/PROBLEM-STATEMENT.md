Required deliverables
Business Model Canvas — customer segments, jobs/pains/gains, value proposition, channels, relationships, activities, resources, partners, costs, and revenue.
Pricing — packaging, price metric, willingness-to-pay evidence, unit economics assumptions, and one rejected alternative.
Working product with strong engineering — a deployed demo or one-command local run; documented architecture; tests; and reliability, security, observability, and data handling appropriate to its risks.
Three to five real users — consent-respecting observations and decision traces in the form hypothesis → evidence → decision. Do not include private personal or client data.
Stakeholder/client interviews — interview script, anonymized notes or summaries, and evidence of what changed. Name an interviewee only with consent.
Roadmap — now/next/later, sequenced by evidence and risk rather than a feature wish list.
Pitch deck — customer problem, insight, product/demo, market, business model, evidence, competition, roadmap, and ask.
Decision log and AI-collaboration disclosure — major decisions, rejected alternatives, model/tool use, verification, and total AI spend.

Grading rubric — 100 points
Product engineering, reliability, and technical depth — 25 points: the core flow works; architecture and trade-offs are clear; meaningful tests cover risk; deployment is reproducible; telemetry and failure handling are appropriate.
Evidence from 3–5 users and decision traces — 15 points: users are real and relevant; traces connect hypotheses to evidence and decisions; negative findings are reported honestly.
Stakeholder/client interviews — 10 points: interview quality, consent, synthesis, and concrete product changes are evident.
Business Model Canvas and commercial logic — 10 points: the canvas is internally coherent, specific, and evidence-backed.
Pricing and go-to-market reasoning — 5 points: packaging, price metric, assumptions, and one alternative are defensible.
Evidence-backed roadmap — 5 points: sequencing reflects validated risk, dependencies, and learning—not just preferences.
Pitch deck and live product narrative — 10 points: concise, credible, and integrated with a working demo and business evidence.
Individual role ownership and cross-functional execution — 10 points: the learner's role evidence shows accountable delivery, sound decisions, and helpful cross-functional work.
Community discussions and decision quality — 10 points: three substantive weekly checkpoints plus constructive responses; posts show evidence, decisions, changed thinking, and professional collaboration.
The first 80 points primarily evaluate the shared team artifact. The final 20 points are individual. Serious individual gaps can also reduce the shared-artifact score where that learner owned the missing deliverable.

Submission
Each learner must upload one ZIP archive. Team members may share the same product bundle, but each archive must also include ROLE_EVIDENCE_<name>.md identifying the learner's contributions and linking to their community posts.

Include the codebase, README, pinned dependency or lock files, one-command run instructions, tests, generated evidence/metrics, Business Model Canvas, pricing, research/interview synthesis, roadmap, pitch deck, decision log, AI-collaboration disclosure, and role evidence.

Do not include secrets, API keys, credentials, .env files, private client data, dependency caches, virtual environments, node_modules, or generated build folders. The archive must be a real ZIP and remain below 500 MB uncompressed.

Integrity and professional bar
Do not fabricate users, interviews, metrics, or outcomes. Disclose AI use and verify generated work. Obtain consent for client participation and anonymize sensitive material. Credit third-party assets and respect trademarks, licenses, and source terms.
