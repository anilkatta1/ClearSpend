# Anonymized interview summaries

All summaries are consented, anonymized, and edited for clarity. Consent to share was received on 18 September. They should be read as qualitative evidence, not quantified outcome evidence.

## I-01 — Finance-team reviewer: reimbursement review

The participant described a reimbursement process where multiple reviewers repeatedly download receipts, compare merchant/date/amount details, locate policy text, and contact budget owners or earlier approvers. The resulting rationale can be fragmented across messages and documents.

The participant considered a unified review packet useful when it presents receipt previews, policy context and explanations, and a recorded recommendation alongside the human decision. They specifically valued avoiding individual receipt downloads, preserving an audit trail, retaining human authority, and allowing missing information to be appended to the same case rather than restarting it.

**Product implications:** retain inline preview, pinned policy citations, immutable evidence/history, human decision ownership, and append/resubmit workflow.

**Limit:** this is an edited feedback summary, not a measurement of time saved or a commitment to buy.

## I-02 — Operations leader: internal operating expenses

The participant described quarterly budget planning in a spreadsheet, requisitions and invoice attachments in a ticketing system, and project/client cost mapping in a project-cost system. Fixed facilities costs, routine operating expenses, and project-linked spending follow different approval paths. Routine spend is ticket-first; project spend is linked to a project code and requires project and operations sign-off. Weekly reconciliation and monthly variance review are used to compare operational records with the budget baseline; material variance prompts review.

**Product implications:** reimbursement evidence should retain budget/policy context, route exceptions to a human, and support an auditable reconciliation trail rather than claiming to replace organizational approval or budget systems.

**Limit:** this is process discovery. The participant did not evaluate ClearSpend, so it is not evidence of product preference or willingness to pay.

## I-03 — IT procurement leader: asset procure-to-pay

The participant described a controlled asset-purchasing flow: management justification and approval, a purchase request and competing quotations, purchase-order issuance, delivery/specification verification, invoice payment, and debit/credit-note reconciliation for returns or changes.

**Product implications:** preserve a traceable chain between authorization, evidence, verification, and adjustment. Do not infer that ClearSpend supports purchase orders, procurement, or asset management; its current scope is reimbursement review.

**Limit:** this is adjacent-domain process discovery, not a ClearSpend usability study.

## I-04 — Relocation reimbursement stakeholder: employee logistics expenses

The participant described relocation support that begins with an approved cap during onboarding, uses approved vendors, requires itemized invoices and proof of payment, and proceeds through a reimbursement ticket and multi-role review before finance settlement.

**Product implications:** a reimbursement workflow needs evidence capture, a policy/budget reference, receipt quality checks, and a transparent human-review record. Approved-vendor enforcement, onboarding eligibility, and payment remain organization-owned controls outside the current MVP.

**Limit:** the notes describe a workflow rather than measured outcomes or a product evaluation.

## I-05 — Frequent employee reimbursement submitter: workflow and product feedback

The participant regularly submits receipt-based reimbursement claims through an existing multi-step process. They described repeated receipt downloads and checks, fragmented policy, budget, and approval context, delays while evidence passes between approvers, and difficulty reconstructing a decision later. They considered a single packet containing receipt previews, confirmed fields, policy results, explanations, and decision history valuable. They also valued retaining human review and continuing the same claim when a reviewer requests missing evidence rather than restarting it.

**Product implications:** retain unified evidence, inline receipt preview, deterministic line matching, visible policy context, human decision ownership, append/resubmit workflow, and auditable history.

**Limit:** this is consented, independent qualitative feedback from one frequent submitting user. It supports workflow relevance and product direction; it does not measure reviewer time saved, willingness to pay, or production readiness.
